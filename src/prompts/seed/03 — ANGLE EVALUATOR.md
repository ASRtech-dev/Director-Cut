EVALUATION PRINCIPLES

\============================================================

Use the following principles.

\------------------------------------------------------------

1\. POSITION BEATS TOPIC

\------------------------------------------------------------

An angle must argue something.

Weak:

“This video is about family trauma.”

Strong:

“This story is frightening because it shows how family trauma becomes normal when everyone depends on not naming it.”

Reject or downgrade any angle that is only a theme, topic, or area of interest.

\------------------------------------------------------------

2\. SPECIFIC WOUND BEATS ABSTRACT THEME

\------------------------------------------------------------

A strong emotional core is specific.

Weak:

“This is about fear.”

Strong:

“This is about the fear that love does not automatically make someone safe.”

Weak:

“This is about identity.”

Strong:

“This is about the terror of realizing the role that protected you has also trapped you.”

Downgrade any angle whose emotional wound is vague.

\------------------------------------------------------------

3\. CLOSE ANALYSIS BEATS CLEVERNESS

\------------------------------------------------------------

A clever thesis is not enough.

The angle must be supportable through:

\- scenes;

\- passages;

\- symbols;

\- decisions;

\- images;

\- dialogue;

\- formal choices;

\- historical context;

\- adaptation differences;

\- recurring motifs.

If an angle sounds brilliant but lacks evidence, mark it as risky.

\------------------------------------------------------------

4\. RETENTION BEATS COMPLETENESS

\------------------------------------------------------------

A video essay is not an encyclopedia.

An angle should create movement.

It should allow:

\- escalation;

\- revelation;

\- contrast;

\- midpoint turn;

\- emotional deepening;

\- visual variety;

\- argument progression.

Downgrade angles that feel static, even if they are intellectually correct.

\------------------------------------------------------------

5\. VOICE BEATS NEUTRALITY

\------------------------------------------------------------

The best angle should invite a distinctive narrator voice.

It should allow sharp, stylish, darkly funny, emotionally intelligent narration.

Downgrade angles that would force the video into neutral explanation, school essay tone, or generic review language.

\------------------------------------------------------------

6\. VISUAL POTENTIAL MATTERS

\------------------------------------------------------------

A strong angle should generate visual material.

Look for:

\- recurring objects;

\- symbolic places;

\- character contrasts;

\- physical rituals;

\- visual motifs;

\- cinematic moments;

\- documents;

\- rooms;

\- machines;

\- costumes;

\- architecture;

\- landscapes;

\- body language;

\- public/private contrast.

A good script can still fail as a video if it is visually thin.

\------------------------------------------------------------

7\. PACKAGING IS NOT SHALLOW

\------------------------------------------------------------

Title and thumbnail potential matter.

A strong video essay angle should be communicable in a compelling title without cheap clickbait.

Downgrade angles that are excellent internally but impossible to package clearly.

\------------------------------------------------------------

8\. EXECUTION RISK MUST BE HONEST

\------------------------------------------------------------

Identify risks clearly.

Possible risks:

\- too obvious;

\- too abstract;

\- too academic;

\- too narrow;

\- too broad;

\- too dependent on one metaphor;

\- too similar to existing common interpretations;

\- too hard to support;

\- too difficult to title;

\- too emotionally flat;

\- too visually thin;

\- too dependent on adaptation comparison;

\- too morally simplistic;

\- too clever for its own good.

Do not hide weaknesses.

SCORING RUBRIC

\============================================================

After the multi-role evaluation, create a scoring table.

Score each angle from 1 to 10 in each category.

Use decimal scores if useful.

Categories and weights:

1\. Thesis strength — 15%

Does the angle argue something specific and compelling?

2\. Originality — 10%

Does the angle avoid obvious or overused readings?

3\. Emotional wound — 12%

Does it touch a precise human fear, shame, desire, grief, or contradiction?

4\. Analytical depth — 13%

Can it support serious close reading or cultural analysis?

5\. Retention potential — 12%

Can it hold attention for 15 minutes?

6\. Visual potential — 10%

Can it generate strong visual support?

7\. Voice potential — 10%

Can it support a distinctive, stylish, darkly funny narrator?

8\. Packaging potential — 8%

Can it become a compelling title/thumbnail without cheapness?

9\. Midpoint/counterpoint potential — 5%

Can the argument deepen and then be tested?

10\. Execution safety — 5%

How likely is it to be executed well without becoming vague, generic, or forced?

Calculate a weighted score out of 100.

Do not compress all scores into the same range.

Create meaningful differences.
