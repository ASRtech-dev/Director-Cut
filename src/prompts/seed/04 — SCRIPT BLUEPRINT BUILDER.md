BLUEPRINT PRINCIPLES

\============================================================

Use the following principles.

\------------------------------------------------------------

1\. THESIS CONTROLS EVERYTHING

\------------------------------------------------------------

The thesis is the spine.

Every section must serve it.

Do not include interesting material unless it:

\- advances the thesis;

\- complicates the thesis;

\- deepens the thesis;

\- provides evidence for the thesis;

\- or prepares the emotional landing.

If a fact, scene, symbol, or comparison is interesting but not useful, exclude it.

\------------------------------------------------------------

2\. ARGUMENT FIRST, TOPICS SECOND

\------------------------------------------------------------

The structure must not be a list of themes.

Weak structure:

\- Talk about the author.

\- Talk about the main character.

\- Talk about symbolism.

\- Talk about the ending.

Strong structure:

\- Establish the common misconception.

\- Show how the main character already carries the damage the world later amplifies.

\- Reveal how the setting turns private weakness into public structure.

\- Use the midpoint to argue that the monster is not outside the system but produced by it.

\- Test the thesis through a counterpoint.

\- Land on the human wound underneath the story.

Every section must be an argumentative movement.

\------------------------------------------------------------

3\. SPECIFICITY BEATS ELEGANCE

\------------------------------------------------------------

The blueprint must identify specific evidence.

Depending on the subject, include:

\- scenes;

\- passages;

\- lines;

\- character decisions;

\- symbols;

\- settings;

\- formal choices;

\- adaptation differences;

\- historical events;

\- creator decisions;

\- public reactions;

\- cultural artifacts;

\- recurring motifs.

A future script cannot be strong if the blueprint is abstract.

\------------------------------------------------------------

4\. MIDPOINT TURN IS MANDATORY

\------------------------------------------------------------

The future script needs a clear midpoint turn.

The midpoint is where the video becomes more interesting than the viewer expected.

It should deepen the argument, not contradict it.

It should not be a cheap twist.

It should reveal the deeper mechanism underneath the visible thesis.

Good midpoint turn examples:

\- “The deeper horror is not that the system fails. It is that the system works exactly as designed.”

\- “The monster is not the opposite of the family. It is the family’s damage given a place to live.”

\- “This is where the story stops being about survival and becomes about the cost of becoming survivable.”

\------------------------------------------------------------

5\. COUNTERPOINT MUST BE REAL

\------------------------------------------------------------

The counterpoint section must test the thesis.

Do not invent a fake objection.

The counterpoint may come from:

\- an alternative reading;

\- an adaptation;

\- a limitation of the thesis;

\- a moral ambiguity;

\- a formal weakness;

\- a public interpretation;

\- an unresolved contradiction.

The response should make the argument more intelligent, not merely louder.

\------------------------------------------------------------

6\. VISUAL LOGIC MATTERS

\------------------------------------------------------------

The blueprint must identify visual anchors.

A video essay needs images, symbols, contrasts, objects, spaces, bodies, rituals, documents, colors, machines, architecture, landscapes, or visual rhythms.

A good thesis should be visually supportable.

If a section is visually thin, flag it.

Do not write production directions yet.

Instead, identify the visual logic and potential visual anchors.

\------------------------------------------------------------

7\. NARRATOR VOICE MUST BE DISTINCTIVE

\------------------------------------------------------------

Define the narrator’s operating mode.

The future script should not sound neutral.

Choose or refine one:

\- The Elegant Knife

\- The Cultural Coroner

\- The Haunted Professor

\- The Noir Essayist

\- The Emotional Surgeon

\- The Absurd Historian

Describe how the narrator should speak:

\- sentence rhythm;

\- humor style;

\- emotional temperature;

\- metaphor family;

\- level of irony;

\- level of intimacy.

The narrator should have a point of view.

BLOCK 2 — ARGUMENT SPINE

\============================================================

Create a 7–10 step argument spine.

Each step must be an argumentative movement, not a topic.

For each step, include:

1\. Step title

2\. Core claim

3\. Why this step is necessary

4\. Evidence to use

5\. Emotional or intellectual effect on the viewer

6\. Transition into the next step

The argument spine should show clear escalation.

The viewer should feel the video moving:

\- from surface;

\- to contradiction;

\- to mechanism;

\- to deeper wound;

\- to counterpoint;

\- to final resonance.

BLOCK 4 — HOOK BLUEPRINT

\============================================================

Develop 3 hook strategies.

Do not write full hooks yet unless useful.

For each hook strategy, include:

1\. Hook type

Choose:

\- Dark, Strange, or Unsettling

\- Sharp, Ironic, or Intellectually Provocative

\- Emotional, Intimate, or Philosophical

2\. Opening tension

What curiosity gap does the first line create?

3\. First-line options

Give 3 possible first lines.

4\. How the subject is revealed

Explain how the topic enters naturally within 20–35 seconds.

5\. First punchline or memorable turn

Give 2–3 possible lines.

6\. Risk

How this hook could fail.

7\. Best use case

When this hook should be chosen.

Then recommend the strongest hook direction.

BLOCK 5 — EVIDENCE MAP

\============================================================

Create a map of required evidence.

Include 10–15 pieces of evidence from the research.

For each evidence item, include:

1\. Evidence item

Scene, passage, symbol, object, decision, historical detail, adaptation difference, public reaction, etc.

2\. What it proves

How it supports or complicates the thesis.

3\. Where to use it

Hook, functional presentation, context, analysis, midpoint, counterpoint, closing.

4\. Emotional value

What it makes the viewer feel.

5\. Visual value

What kind of visual material or mental image it offers.

6\. Risk

How it could be misused, overexplained, or become irrelevant.

\============================================================

BLOCK 6 — MIDPOINT TURN DESIGN

\============================================================

Design the midpoint carefully.

Include:

1\. Visible thesis before midpoint

What the viewer thinks the argument is so far.

2\. Deeper thesis after midpoint

What the argument becomes.

3\. Midpoint hinge sentence

Write 5 possible hinge sentences.

4\. Evidence that triggers the turn

Which scene, symbol, event, contradiction, or formal choice should produce the turn?

5\. Emotional shift

How the viewer should feel before and after the turn.

6\. Retention function

Why this turn renews attention.

7\. What to avoid

How not to make the midpoint feel like a cheap twist.

\============================================================

BLOCK 7 — COUNTERPOINT DESIGN

\============================================================

Design the counterpoint.

Include:

1\. Strongest objection or complication

State it in its strongest form.

2\. Why the objection is valid or tempting

Explain why an intelligent viewer might accept it.

3\. Evidence supporting the counterpoint

Specific evidence.

4\. Response to the counterpoint

How the video should answer, absorb, or reframe it.

5\. Revised stronger thesis after counterpoint

One sentence.

6\. Emotional effect

How the counterpoint should deepen trust.

7\. What to avoid

Fake balance, weak objections, or over-defensiveness.

1\. Thesis lines

2\. Character lines

4\. Darkly funny lines

5\. Emotional wound lines

The lines should be sharp, but they must serve the argument.

No empty slogans.

No trailer copy.

No artificial profundity.
