EVALUATION PRINCIPLES

\============================================================

Use the following principles.

\------------------------------------------------------------

1\. THESIS FIDELITY

\------------------------------------------------------------

A version is strong only if it preserves the approved thesis.

Downgrade any version that:

\- changes the thesis;

\- weakens the thesis;

\- becomes too broad;

\- becomes a generic analysis;

\- loses the emotional wound;

\- overemphasizes a secondary idea until the video becomes unfocused.

\------------------------------------------------------------

2\. STRUCTURE MATTERS

\------------------------------------------------------------

A strong script must move.

Look for:

\- strong opening;

\- clear orientation;

\- useful context;

\- escalating analysis;

\- real midpoint turn;

\- genuine counterpoint;

\- earned emotional closing.

Downgrade any version that becomes:

\- a list of themes;

\- a beautiful but static essay;

\- a long plot explanation;

\- a collection of good lines without progression.

\------------------------------------------------------------

3\. VOICE MATTERS

\------------------------------------------------------------

A strong script must sound distinctive.

It should not sound:

\- neutral;

\- generic;

\- academic;

\- robotic;

\- overly polished but lifeless;

\- like a Wikipedia article;

\- like a school essay;

\- like a comedy routine.

Reward versions that sound:

\- intelligent;

\- stylish;

\- darkly funny;

\- emotionally precise;

\- alive;

\- suitable for spoken narration.

\------------------------------------------------------------

4\. EVIDENCE MATTERS

\------------------------------------------------------------

A strong script must use concrete evidence.

Reward scripts that use:

\- specific scenes;

\- passages;

\- symbols;

\- decisions;

\- objects;

\- adaptation contrasts;

\- historical context;

\- recurring motifs;

\- formal choices.

Downgrade scripts that rely on abstraction without proof.

\------------------------------------------------------------

5\. RETENTION MATTERS

\------------------------------------------------------------

A strong video essay must hold attention.

Reward:

\- curiosity gaps;

\- paragraph landings;

\- tonal variation;

\- sharp transitions;

\- concrete images;

\- memorable lines;

\- emotional turns;

\- midpoint deepening;

\- controlled humor.

Downgrade:

\- long flat passages;

\- repeated ideas;

\- predictable analysis;

\- overly dense paragraphs;

\- lack of surprise;

\- lack of spoken rhythm.

\------------------------------------------------------------

6\. VISUAL POTENTIAL MATTERS

\------------------------------------------------------------

A script can be intellectually strong but visually weak.

Reward scripts that naturally suggest:

\- recurring images;

\- symbolic objects;

\- strong scenes;

\- visual contrasts;

\- architecture;

\- rituals;

\- faces;

\- gestures;

\- landscapes;

\- documents;

\- machines;

\- spaces;

\- edit-friendly moments.

Downgrade scripts that are purely abstract and hard to support visually.

\------------------------------------------------------------

7\. PUNCH MUST SERVE ARGUMENT

\------------------------------------------------------------

A memorable line is valuable only if it sharpens the thesis.

Reward punchlines that:

\- compress the argument;

\- reveal character contradiction;

\- reframe a symbol;

\- create dark irony;

\- expose emotional truth;

\- land a paragraph with force.

Downgrade lines that feel:

\- artificial;

\- slogan-like;

\- too clever;

\- detached from analysis;

\- repetitive;

\- trailer-ish;

\- emotionally fake.

\------------------------------------------------------------

8\. THE BEST MASTER CUT IS NOT AN AVERAGE

\------------------------------------------------------------

Do not recommend blending everything evenly.

A Master Cut must be selective.

It should:

\- choose the strongest base;

\- import only the best elements from other versions;

\- cut weak material aggressively;

\- preserve a coherent voice;

\- avoid becoming a patchwork.
